The project

An integrated reading of one of the planet's most complex marine systems.

This Work Plan aims to support the Amazon River Mouth Basin project under the "Pelagic System" research line, which covers the zooplankton, ichthyoplankton, neuston and historical fishery landing data groups. The pelagic system is shaped by environmental and climatic factors that make the ecosystem's dynamics even more complex, directly affecting the organisms present.

The Amazon River Mouth Basin is one of the largest and most important basins in the world, standing out for its biodiversity and ecosystem dynamics, which remain little understood. In this context, nutrient availability, organic carbon and suspended particulate matter play a fundamental role in primary productivity and food-web structure, making it essential to study zooplankton and neuston communities using specific techniques and methodologies aimed at environmental characterization.

To this end, five distinct subprojects will be established, whose objectives, rationale, sampling strategies and data/laboratory analysis methodologies are being developed by specialized teams, following a 48-month activity schedule.

The main objectives of this plan include developing a robust and efficient database to store, analyze and forecast fishery landings in the Amazon River Mouth Basin, using both historical and recent data. In addition, the project seeks to characterize the region's zooplankton, ichthyoplankton and neuston communities, with emphasis on the horizontal and vertical distribution of these populations in the water column. Abiotic parameter data (nutrients, suspended particulate matter, particulate organic carbon and nitrogen, dissolved organic carbon) help characterize the pelagic environment.

Additionally, molecular analyses will be carried out using DNA Barcode techniques to support morphological identification of zooplankton organisms, along with stomach-content characterization and quantification of carbon absorption by fish larvae through metabarcoding and stable isotopes, for studies of ecological integration and food webs.

The results obtained will be analyzed according to each research line, generating products that will serve as a reference for the region. In addition, the data will be assessed systemically and integrated with abiotic parameters, contributing to the understanding of zooplankton and neuston community dynamics in the Amazon River Mouth Basin.

Regional context

Equatorial Margin

The Equatorial Margin brings together five sedimentary basins along Brazil's northern and northeastern coast — Foz do Amazonas, Pará-Maranhão, Barreirinhas, Ceará and Potiguar — one of the country's most strategic geological and ecological frontiers. It is within this broader regional context that the PLUMA Project's study area is located.

Scientific territory

Amazon River Mouth Basin

River discharge, macrotides and the North Brazil Current form a hydrographic mosaic that connects the coastal zone, the shelf, the slope and the open ocean. This connectivity regulates nutrients, carbon, productivity and the dispersal of pelagic organisms.

03

Oceanographic campaigns

Multiscale sampling across the Equatorial Margin.

The expeditions combine stations across the shelf, the slope and the open-ocean domain. This strategy makes it possible to assess the horizontal and vertical variability of the pelagic system under different hydrographic and seasonal conditions.
